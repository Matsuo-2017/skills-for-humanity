# How "human-level reasoning" is measured in AI (state as of 29 September 2026): benchmarks, human baselines, critiques, and evaluation harnesses

## Key Question 1: On which benchmarks do frontier models exceed the human baseline, and on which do humans still win, as of September 2026?

### Takeaway
As of September 2026 frontier models exceed the published human baseline on static, knowledge-and-derivation style tests (GPQA Diamond by ~24 points, ARC-AGI-1 and now ARC-AGI-2 versus the average-individual baseline, FrontierMath where no meaningful human baseline exists, Watson & Holmes at roughly the top-5% human level), while humans still clearly win on interactive/novel-environment tests (ARC-AGI-3: humans 100% vs AI <1% at launch), everyday-trick reasoning (SimpleBench: 81.9% vs 83.7%), multimodal expert questions (MMMU: ~80% vs 88.6%), long-horizon economic agency (Vending-Bench 2: ~13-25% of a "good human" strategy; Remote Labor Index: 0.8-2.5% automation), and expert-level open-domain questions (HLE: best ~46-61% depending on harness vs. an implied expert level that is itself contested). The picture is therefore not "human-level reached" but "human-level on benchmarks that reward stored knowledge and short-horizon derivation; sub-human on novelty, interaction, calibration and long horizons."

- Greg Kamradt (ARC Prize Foundation president) stated that at >95%, ARC-AGI-1 "is effectively performance-saturated at this point," but "still has useful life" because performance comes at a cost (undated X post surfaced in Sep 2026 search) — [Greg Kamradt on X](https://x.com/GregKamradt/status/2021997415436558451)

**ARC-AGI-2 (maintainer: ARC Prize Foundation; released March 2025)**
- Dataset: 1,000 public training tasks, 120 public evaluation tasks, plus two private test sets. Human calibration: "Average human performance on these tasks in our test sample was 66%"; every evaluation task "has been solved by a minimum of 2 people ... in 2 attempts or less in a controlled test." Scoring is pass@2 with exact-match grids. License Apache-2.0 — [arcprize/ARC-AGI-2 on GitHub](https://github.com/arcprize/ARC-AGI-2)
- Two distinct human baselines are in circulation: "human panel" completion rate 100% (every task solved by at least two testers) versus average individual performance 66%; each task was attempted by 2-10 non-expert members of the public (snippet) — [ARC-AGI-2 paper, arXiv 2505.11831](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11831); [LessWrong, "ARC-AGI-2 human baseline surpassed (updated)"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DX3EmhmwZjTYp9PBf/ai-performance-has-surpassed-a-human-baseline-on-arc-agi-2)
- Top scores: GPT-5.4 scored 73.3% base / 83.3% Pro at its March 2026 release (snippet); as of July 2026 GPT-5.6 Sol leads at 92.5%, Claude Opus 5 90.4%, GPT-5.5 85% (aggregator, unverified against maintainer) — [BenchLM ARC-AGI-2](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/arcAgi2); [IntuitionLabs GPT-5.2 analysis](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gpt-5-2-arc-agi-2-benchmark)
- Chollet's framing (Dec 2025 X post, snippet): "With test-time adaptation, reasoning models are now capable of genuine fluid intelligence," but "SotA models are not yet human-level on an efficiency basis" and "ARC 2 remains largely unsaturated" — [Francois Chollet on X](https://x.com/fchollet/status/1999183528492433672)
- ARC Prize 2025 results and technical report (Jan 2026) describe the Kaggle-track (compute-limited, offline) results as distinct from the public "verified" frontier-model leaderboard — [ARC Prize 2025 Technical Report, arXiv 2601.10904](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10904v1); [ARC Prize 2025 results blog](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis)

**ARC-AGI-3 (maintainer: ARC Prize Foundation; launched 25 March 2026)**
- Launched 25 March 2026 at Y Combinator, San Francisco. "Humans score 100%. Frontier AI scores 0.51%" per one write-up; another reports GPT-5.4, Claude Opus 4.6 and Grok 4.2 "between 0% and 0.37%" (snippets; secondary sources summarising the ARC Prize launch post) — [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/skilaai/arc-agi-3-just-broke-every-frontier-model-humans-score-100-gpt-54-scores-026-52ah); [ARC Prize launch blog](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-agi-3-launch); [Technical report arXiv 2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621)
- What it tests: "interactive benchmark for studying agentic intelligence through novel, abstract, turn-based environments in which agents must explore, infer goals, build internal models of environment dynamics, and plan effective action sequences without explicit instructions" (snippet of technical report abstract) — [ARC-AGI-3 technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621v1)

- "A 100% score means AI agents can beat every game as efficiently as humans" (snippet) — [arcprize.org/arc-agi/3](https://arcprize.org/arc-agi/3)
- The official benchmarking harness exposes 25 games ("View available games (there should be 25)"), MIT licence — [arcprize/arc-agi-3-benchmarking](https://github.com/arcprize/arc-agi-3-benchmarking)
- Competition: hosted on Kaggle as ARC Prize 2026 with "no internet access during evaluation"; prize-eligible solutions must open-source code; Milestone #1 (30 June 2026) prizes $25K/$10K/$2.5K (snippet) — [Kaggle ARC Prize 2026 ARC-AGI-3](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/arc-prize-2026-arc-agi-3/leaderboard); [ARC Prize 2026 competition page](https://arcprize.org/competitions/2026/arc-agi-3)

- Human leaderboard exists at arcprize.org/arc-agi/3/leaderboard (page blocked; contents not verified) — [ARC-AGI-3 Human Leaderboard](https://arcprize.org/arc-agi/3/leaderboard)
- Interpretation from ARC Prize commentary: "when 0.37% is the world's best score and 100% of humans succeed ... current systems do not yet possess what Chollet calls fluid intelligence" (secondary) — [Anthem Creation summary](https://anthemcreation.com/en/artificial-intelligence/arc-agi-3-why-every-ai-fails-the-intelligence-test/)

**2026 benchmarks built explicitly to compare AI with human reasoning**
- Watson & Holmes (Feb 2026, arXiv 2602.19914): adaptation of a detective tabletop game with incremental narrative evidence, open-ended questions, an automated grader validated against human assessors, and "direct human calibration under matched conditions." Results: best completion model GPT-4.1 scored 1.45 vs human mean 1.43; best reasoning models o3-pro 2.14 and GPT-5 2.10, "estimated near the top 5% of humans." Over nine months of 2025 models moved "from the lower quartile of the human comparison group to approximately the top 5%," about half from steady progress and half from a step change with reasoning-oriented models — [arXiv 2602.19914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.19914); [ResearchGate copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401131988_Watson_Holmes_A_Naturalistic_Benchmark_for_Comparing_Human_and_LLM_Reasoning); [benchmark website repo](https://github.com/Watson-Holmes-Benchmark/Watson-Holmes-Website)

- GDPval (OpenAI; ICLR 2026): 44 occupations across the nine top US GDP sectors, >=30 tasks per occupation built from real expert work products; primary metric is head-to-head human expert comparison; automated grader "agrees with human experts less often than humans agree with each other (a ~5-point gap)" — [GDPval ICLR 2026 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/290c2430f91912204f30bbcc990fff1d-Paper-Conference.pdf); [GDPval arXiv 2510.04374](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04374)

### Inferences
- The "human baseline" is not a single thing. Benchmarks use at least five different constructions: (a) panel-solvability (ARC-AGI-2/3: every task solved by >=2 people, giving "100%"), (b) mean individual accuracy of a small sample (ARC-AGI-2 66%, SimpleBench 83.7% n=9, MMMU 88.6%), (c) domain-expert accuracy (GPQA ~70%), (d) a theoretical "good strategy" (Vending-Bench 2 ~$63k), and (e) head-to-head expert preference (GDPval, RLI). Claims that AI is "above human level" are only meaningful relative to the specific construction, and the ARC-AGI-2 case shows the same benchmark can be "surpassed" (vs 66% individual mean) and "not surpassed" (vs 100% panel, on efficiency) at once.
- The benchmarks where humans still win share a structure: novelty at test time (ARC-AGI-3), interaction with an environment over many steps (Vending-Bench 2, RLI), or adversarially simple distractors (SimpleBench). The benchmarks AI now dominates reward recall plus short derivation with a verifiable answer.

## Key Question 2: What does the METR time-horizon trend show as of 2026?

### Takeaway
METR's 50%-success time horizon for frontier models grew from about 4h49m (Claude Opus 4.5, late 2025) to "likely at least 16 hours" (Claude Mythos Preview, May 2026 dashboard update), with METR's Time Horizon 1.1 (29 Jan 2026) finding an 89-day doubling time since 2024 versus 7 months over 2019-2025; however the 80% horizon lagged badly (27 min for Opus 4.5; 3h06m for Mythos Preview), METR says measurements above 16 hours are unreliable on its current task suite, and both METR and outside critics warn against reading the metric as "human-level."

## Key Question 3: What are the strongest published critiques of using benchmarks to claim human-level reasoning?

### Takeaway
The strongest critiques cluster into six lines: (1) benchmark item quality is poor (29% of HLE bio/chem answers wrong; 42% of FrontierMath problems needed correction); (2) human baselines are tiny or ill-defined (SimpleBench n=9, MATH n=1, HLE no aggregate); (3) benchmark scores do not predict real-task performance (a 37% lab-to-deployment gap for agents; RLI automation 0.8-2.5% while static scores exceed 90%); (4) construct validity problems, especially for theory of mind and commonsense; (5) contamination and conflict-of-interest (FrontierMath funding, monthly-rotation benchmarks as the response); and (6) brittleness under perturbation and overconfidence (calibration errors of 34-89% on HLE, large ID-OOD drops). The International AI Safety Report 2026 endorses the "evaluation gap" framing at the policy level.

### Cited Findings
- International AI Safety Report 2026 (chaired by Yoshua Bengio; published Feb 2026): reasoning models produced the largest jumps in maths, software and science, "gold-medal-level performance on International Mathematical Olympiad questions and answered more than 80% of graduate-level science questions," yet models "still fail at elementary mathematical calculations and struggle to infer basic causal relationships," are "less reliable when projects involve many steps," and the report names an "evaluation gap" between controlled tests and real-world performance — [International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026); [arXiv 2602.21012](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.21012); [Inside Global Tech summary](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2026/02/10/international-ai-safety-report-2026-examines-ai-capabilities-risks-and-safeguards/)
- Item quality: FutureHouse found ~29% of HLE text-only chem/bio answers wrong or misleading (chemistry 57.0% contradicted by literature in the flagged subset, biology/health 51.6%), attributing it to a selection protocol that kept only questions frontier models failed and gave reviewers five minutes each; the HLE team's own re-audit found ~18% problematic — [FutureHouse](https://www.futurehouse.org/research/hle-exam); [LavX summary](https://news.lavx.hu/article/humanity-s-last-exam-under-scrutiny-study-finds-29-of-ai-benchmark-s-bio-chem-questions-contradicted-by-research)
- Item quality: Epoch found errors in 42% of FrontierMath problems and shipped v2 on 12 June 2026 — [Epoch FrontierMath Tier 4 v2](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath-tier-4-v2)
- Independence: OpenAI's undisclosed funding of and access to FrontierMath (only a verbal no-training agreement; holdout set) — [TechCrunch, Jan 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/19/ai-benchmarking-organization-criticized-for-waiting-to-disclose-funding-from-openai)
- Predictive validity: "Benchmarking the Benchmarks: Testing the Predictive Validity of Commonsense Benchmarks" (Aug 2026, arXiv 2608.03340) tests whether commonsense benchmark scores predict downstream reasoning (paper blocked; only title and existence verified) — [arXiv 2608.03340](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.03340)
- Lab-to-deployment gap: "enterprise agentic AI systems show a 37% gap between lab benchmark scores and real-world deployment performance, with 50x cost variation for similar accuracy" (snippet; secondary industry source, methodology not verified) — [Kili Technology 2026 guide](https://kili-technology.com/blog/ai-benchmarks-guide-the-top-evaluations-in-2026-and-why-theyre-not-enough)
- Saturation: "MMLU and MMLU-Pro are functionally saturated above 88% for frontier AI models, making score differences at the top statistically meaningless" (snippet) — [Kili Technology 2026 guide](https://kili-technology.com/blog/ai-benchmarks-guide-the-top-evaluations-in-2026-and-why-theyre-not-enough)
- Human-baseline quality: "Some baselines rest on very small samples (SimpleBench n=9, MATH n=1)" — [Kaggle dataset, AI Benchmarks vs Human Baselines](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/kylefengkfeng209/ai-benchmarks-vs-human-baselines)
- Construct validity (ToM): Riemer et al. (ICML 2025) — ToM benchmarks "fall victim to a fallacy of attributing human-like qualities to AI agents"; literal ToM scores are "only informative when agents exhibit self-consistent reasoning," which LLMs lack; strong literal ToM "does not necessarily imply strong functional theory of mind or vice versa" — [arXiv 2412.19726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19726)
- Construct validity (ToM, user-centred): "Rethinking Theory of Mind Benchmarks for LLMs: Towards a User-Centered Perspective" (Apr 2025) — [arXiv 2504.10839](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.10839v1)
- Process validity: even correct ToM answers do not show LLMs "reason about mental states the same way humans do"; CogToM reports "potential divergences between LLM and human cognitive structures" — [CogToM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15628)
- Brittleness/OOD: ThinkBench (2025) does dynamic out-of-distribution evaluation; formally calibrated probes (2026) "consistently expose robustness gaps across mathematical and scientific reasoning domains, with many models achieving high original accuracy but their performance dropping substantially after randomization"; numeric-remapping attacks (June 2026) test arithmetic generalisation — [ThinkBench arXiv 2502.16268](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.16268); [Mapping LLM Capability Frontiers, arXiv 2603.05290](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05290v2); [Numeric-remapping attacks, arXiv 2606.03606](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03606)
- Calibration: HLE calibration errors 34-89%; 2026 work notes "reasoning models may be confidently wrong, and their expressed uncertainty may not faithfully reflect the quality of the reasoning process" — [Let's Data Science on HLE](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/humanitys-last-exam-the-test-thats-humbling-the-worlds-smartest-ai); [Process Supervision of Confidence Margin, arXiv 2604.23333](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23333v1)
- Reporting practice: "Frontier Lag: A Bibliometric Audit of Capability Misrepresentation in Academic AI Evaluation" (May 2026) audits how academic papers misstate frontier capability (blocked; title only) — [arXiv 2605.04135](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04135); "Evaluation Cards: An Interpretive Layer for AI Evaluation Reporting" (June 2026) proposes standardised reporting — [arXiv 2606.09809](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09809)
- Economic-value benchmarks: GDPval's own grader agrees with experts ~5 points less than experts agree with each other and "is not a full substitute"; Epoch argues such benchmarks "don't fully live up to their names" — [GDPval ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/290c2430f91912204f30bbcc990fff1d-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/what-do-economic-value-benchmarks-tell-us)
- ARC Prize's design critique of static benchmarks: ARC-AGI-3 was built because static tests cannot measure "exploration, modelling, goal-setting, planning" in novel environments, and scores are normalised to human action-efficiency rather than raw accuracy — [ARC-AGI-3 technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24621)
- AGI forecasting field review (Apr 2026) catalogues methodological gaps in using benchmarks for AGI timelines — [arXiv 2604.22766](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22766)

### Inferences
- The most damaging critiques are empirical rather than philosophical: measured error rates in the two flagship "hard" benchmarks (HLE ~18-29% in audited domains, FrontierMath 42%) mean that score movements of 10-20 points in 2025-2026 are within the noise introduced by item errors, and "surpassing" a human on such items is partly measuring who matches the (sometimes wrong) key.
- Contamination has been partially addressed structurally (private test sets for ARC, monthly rotation for LiveBench, holdouts for FrontierMath) rather than statistically; the field has largely stopped claiming static public benchmarks are contamination-free.
- The convergent recommendation across critiques is to report harness, human-baseline construction, calibration and cost alongside accuracy, and to pair static scores with interactive/economic evals.

### Gaps
- The full text of the 2026 critique papers (2608.03340, 2605.04135, 2606.09809, MIT Technology Review piece, METR's limitations note) could not be read; specific quantitative findings from them are not captured.
- The "37% lab-to-deployment gap" figure is from an industry vendor blog and could not be traced to a primary study.

## Key Question 4: What evaluation harnesses (open-source code) can a practitioner use to test a reasoning system themselves?

### Takeaway
Four general-purpose harnesses cover most of the benchmarks above (Inspect/inspect_evals, lm-evaluation-harness, HELM now in maintenance mode, and benchmark-specific repos from ARC Prize, METR, Sierra, DeepMind and the MMMU team); all are MIT or Apache-2.0 licensed and runnable against API models with one command.

### Cited Findings
- **inspect_evals** (Generality Labs, a London nonprofit, with UK AI Security Institute, Arcadia Impact and Vector Institute; MIT): "A library of evaluations built using Inspect AI"; run with `uv run inspect eval inspect_evals/arc_easy --model openai/gpt-5-nano`; includes ARC Easy/Challenge and BBEH among others — [UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_evals](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_evals); [Inspect Evals BBEH page](https://ukgovernmentbeis.github.io/inspect_evals/evals/reasoning/bbeh/)
- **lm-evaluation-harness** (EleutherAI; MIT): "A unified framework to test generative language models on a large number of different evaluation tasks"; 60+ benchmarks, hundreds of subtasks; backend of Hugging Face's Open LLM Leaderboard; example `lm_eval --model hf --model_args pretrained=... --tasks hellaswag`; MMMU listed as a prototype multimodal task — [EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness); [task list](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness/blob/main/lm_eval/tasks/README.md)
- **HELM** (Stanford CRFM; Apache-2.0): standardised datasets including MMLU-Pro, GPQA, IFEval, WildBench; metrics beyond accuracy; `helm-run --run-entries mmlu:subject=philosophy,model=openai/gpt2 --suite my-suite`; "entered maintenance mode on June 1, 2026"; capabilities leaderboard at crfm.stanford.edu/helm/capabilities/latest/ — [stanford-crfm/helm](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm); [HELM Capabilities leaderboard](https://crfm.stanford.edu/helm/capabilities/latest/)

## Key Question 5 (objective item): Expert positions on whether frontier models have reached human-level reasoning, and where the remaining gaps are
