# Best Reasoners Aren't the Most Human

As of 29 September 2026, "best at reasoning tasks" and "most faithful to human reasoning" point to two different stacks, and a builder working on top of Claude Code skills has to choose which one they are building. For task performance, the frontier is a tie between **GPT-6 Astra** (ARC-AGI-2 95%, FrontierMath Tier 4 97.6% lab-reported, GPQA Diamond 96%) and the **Claude 5.x line** (Humanity's Last Exam 67.7% for Opus 5.5 on Anthropic's harness, GDPval-AA 1846, SWE-bench Pro 81.2% for Fable 5.1), with composite indices placing them inside the noise band (Epoch ECI 166 vs 164; Artificial Analysis 53 vs 53); open weights trail by roughly **four months / 8 ECI points**, and the training code behind them has consolidated on six actively maintained RLVR trainers led by verl. For human fidelity, the evidence says the opposite of what a chat-model user would expect: **post-training makes models less human-like**, the best trial-level predictor of human choices is a **Llama-3.1-70B base model fine-tuned on Psych-101 (Centaur)** whose in-distribution fit is matched by 0.6B–1B models, and LLM "digital twins" reproduce population averages while carrying little individual-level signal. Inference-time scaffolds that defined 2023–2025 (manual chain-of-thought, Tree of Thoughts, PRM-guided search, adversarial debate) are documented as obsolete or budget-losing against a single adaptive-thinking agent, which makes the SKILL.md layer the right place to encode *methodology* rather than *search*. The skill-library niche the reader's repo occupies is real but small and unevaluated: skills-for-humanity is the broadest reasoning pack found (171 skills locally; 200+ methods upstream), no reasoning pack has a credible published uplift, and Anthropic shipped a with/without-skill ablation harness (`claude plugin eval`) in September 2026 that makes such an eval cheap to run. Benchmarks say humans still win where novelty, interaction, and long horizons dominate (ARC-AGI-3, SimpleBench, Vending-Bench 2, Remote Labor Index) and lose where recall plus short derivation is enough. A large share of the non-Anthropic numbers in this report come from search snippets of blocked primary pages; those are flagged throughout and listed in the closing section.

## GPT-6 Astra and Claude 5.x co-lead on different benchmark families

Every major closed lab shipped a flagship between June and September 2026, and the September cluster is the one that matters for a simulator built today. Anthropic released **Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1 on 1 September** as "the same model, but with different levels of safeguards," Mythos being restricted to Project Glasswing and US verification programs ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1)); both run 1M-token context, 128K output, always-on adaptive thinking steered by an `effort` parameter, at **$10/$50 per MTok** ([Claude Platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/whats-new-fable-5-1)). **Claude Opus 5.5 followed on 22 September at $4/$20**, 20% below Opus 5, with a fast mode at $8/$40 and "thinking mode cannot be disabled" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)); Anthropic's own routing guidance is to "start with Claude Opus 5.5 for most workloads" and reach for Fable 5.1 "for demanding reasoning and long-horizon agentic work" ([Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)). Sonnet 5.5 shipped 28 September at $2/$10, nearly matching Opus 5.5 on GDPval-AA (1,844 vs 1,846, search summary) ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropics-claude-sonnet-5-5-nearly-matches-opus-5-5-on-benchmarks-while-costing-up-to-30-percent-less-per-task/)). OpenAI's **GPT-6 Astra** went general on 4 September with a "recurrent depth" looped-transformer design that moves part of its reasoning into hidden state ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/9/4/openai-unveils-gpt-6-astra-amid-rising-scrutiny-and-safety); [Raschka](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/gpt-6-astra-looped-transformers-and)), priced at $10/$50 with a 1.05M context (search summary) ([llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/blog/research/gpt-6-astra-launch)), and classified "Critical" for cybersecurity with enterprise rollout off by default ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/09/gpt-6-astra-critical-cyber/)). Google's last Pro-tier release remains Gemini 3.1 Pro from February (ARC-AGI-2 77.1% verified) and Gemini 3 Deep Think (84.6% ARC-AGI-2 at the time), with only Flash-tier models since ([llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/blog/research/gemini-3.1-pro-launch); [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/gemini-3-deep-think-reasoning-benchmarks-guide)); xAI's Grok 4.7 (21 September) and Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 (2 September) sit below the leaders on composite indices ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/x-ai/grok-4.7); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/meta-says-muse-spark-1-3-has-frontier-performance-but-its-best-results-come-from-a-model-developers-cant-broadly-use-yet)).

The two leaders split the reasoning landscape by benchmark family rather than by rank. Astra owns the abstract-puzzle and mathematics tail: **ARC-AGI-2 95.0%** and **ARC-AGI-3 62.7%** on the aggregator snapshot of 24 September ([BenchLM ARC-AGI-2](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/arc-agi-2); [BenchLM ARC-AGI-3](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/arcagi3)), GPQA Diamond 96.0–96.1% ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/aagpqadiamond)), and a lab-reported **97.6% on FrontierMath Tier 4 v2** that Epoch had not independently run; the highest Epoch-run Tier 4 score was 87.8% for Claude Fable 5.1 and Fable 5 ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/frontiermathv2tier4); [TechJack](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/frontiermath-tier-4-and-the-verification-gap-how-to-evaluate/)). The Claude line owns expert knowledge and long-horizon agentic work: on Anthropic's 22 September table, Opus 5.5 scores **HLE 67.7% vs Astra's 57.2%**, GDPval-AA v2.1 1846 vs 1542, Terminal-Bench 4.0 66.4 vs 57.9, while Astra leads Terminal-Bench-Science (64.6 vs 58.7) and AutomationBench (41.4 vs 40.0) ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)). Independent composites cannot separate them: Epoch's Capabilities Index puts Astra at **166 vs Fable 5.1 at 164** where roughly 10 points is the significance threshold (search summary) ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/gpt-6-astra-epoch-capabilities-index/)), and Artificial Analysis v4.3 tied them at 53, with Astra reaching that score at **~40% of Fable 5.1's cost per task ($3.26 vs $7.63)** and roughly a third of the output tokens (27k vs 78k) ([Trending Topics](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/gpt-6-artificial-analysis-update/)). A later aggregator snapshot on an apparently rescaled AA index lists GPT-5.6 Sol 58.9 and Opus 5.5 57.6 and omits Astra and Fable 5.1 entirely, which could not be reconciled without opening the blocked AA page ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/artificialanalysis)). Cost-adjusted, Astra is the stronger pure reasoner; on knowledge work Claude is, and Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 undercuts both by 2.5x on list price.

Two control-surface facts constrain any scaffold built on these models. First, the industry has converged on discrete effort levels with adaptive allocation inside each level: on Fable 5.1 both `budget_tokens` and `thinking: disabled` return HTTP 400, and forced tool use is rejected "because thinking is always on for these models, and a forced tool call would skip it" ([Claude Platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/whats-new-fable-5-1)). Second, **no vendor returns the raw chain of thought**: Claude returns omitted-by-default or summarized thinking plus model-bound encrypted blocks, Gemini returns thought summaries plus signatures, and Astra's system card acknowledges that chain-of-thought monitorability is lower than earlier models because of "shorter, less informative reasoning traces" (search summary) ([Kingy AI](https://kingy.ai/blog/recurrent-depth-openai-astra/); [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/top-ai-models-apis-flaw-exposes-hidden-reasoning/)). An August 2026 paper showed the encrypted reasoning envelopes of all three vendors could be replayed into a cheaper sibling model to print the hidden trace in plaintext, and Fable 5.1's one-directional thinking-block binding is consistent with a mitigation ([arXiv 2608.09867](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.09867v1); [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/openai-anthropic-google-api-flaw-let.html)). For a *simulator* this matters directly: whatever "reasoning process" the system exposes to users will have to be produced by the scaffold and the skills, not read off the model.

## Open weights trail by four months while the training stack consolidates

The open-weight frontier in September 2026 is held by very large sparse mixtures of experts from Chinese labs: Zhipu **GLM-5.3 (753B)**, Moonshot **Kimi K3 (2.8T total, 104B active)**, Xiaomi **MiMo-V2.6-Pro (>1T, MIT, released 22 September with its RL code and environments)**, Alibaba **Qwen3.8-Max (2.4T-A95B, Apache 2.0)** and **DeepSeek V4-Pro (1.6T-A49B, MIT)**, with NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra (550B hybrid Mamba-Transformer) the strongest US entrant ([TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/09/22/xiaomi-open-sources-mimo-v2-6-models-after-scaling-reinforcement-learning/); [Qwen blog](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8); [NVIDIA](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-debuts-nemotron-3-family-of-open-models)). Epoch measures the open–closed gap since January 2026 at **four months, or about 8 ECI points**, "similar to the gap between GPT-5 and GPT-5.5" ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/open-closed-eci-gap)); Artificial Analysis put the gap at 4 index points when Kimi K3 launched in July ([AA on X](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2081926991788626011)) and aggregators report it widening to about 9 after Astra shipped ([ibl.ai](https://ibl.ai/blog/open-weight-models-close-the-gap-government-procurement), aggregator). The gap is not uniform: on verifiable-answer evals the best open models are at parity (Kimi K3 GPQA Diamond 93.5% vs Fable 5's 92.6%, AIME 2025 96.1%), but on Humanity's Last Exam K3 scores 43.5 against Fable 5's 53.3 ([DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/blog/kimi-k3-vs-claude-opus-4-8-vs-gpt-5-6-sol), aggregator). Meta has left the open-weight top tier: the Llama line ended with Llama 4, Muse Spark is closed, and the open Muse Glimmer 30B sits at an AA index of 35 ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/goodbye-llama-meta-launches-new-proprietary-ai-model-muse-spark-first-since); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/meta-open-weight-ai-9.7301484)). DeepSeek R2 never shipped and the R-series has been folded into V4's reasoning mode ([layer3labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/deepseek-r2-explained), aggregator).

Licensing has swung toward permissive terms, with custom clauses reserved for the two largest checkpoints. Qwen 3.5–3.8, Gemma 4, gpt-oss, Mistral Large 3 and Muse Glimmer are Apache 2.0; DeepSeek V4, GLM-5 through 5.2, GLM-5.3-Flash and MiMo-V2.6 are MIT; Nemotron 3 uses the Linux Foundation's OpenMDW-1.1. The exceptions are the Kimi K3 License (a separate agreement above $20M/yr model-as-a-service revenue and mandatory "Kimi K3" display above 100M MAU) and the bespoke GLM-5.3 License (Z.AI security review for providers above $10B revenue) ([morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/kimi-k3); [digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/glm-5-3-weights-bespoke-license-not-mit); both aggregator, license texts unread). For a simulator that must run locally, the credible floor for multi-step reasoning has dropped to 9–30B: **Qwen3.5-9B scores GPQA Diamond 81.7 in 5.5 GB at Q4** ([apxml](https://apxml.com/models/qwen35-9b), aggregator), gpt-oss-20b fits in 16 GB ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/welcome-openai-gpt-oss)), and Gemma 4 31B or Muse Glimmer 30B fit a 24 GB card; the 3B claims (VibeThinker-3B at 94.3 on AIME 2026) are contested ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/why-weibos-tiny-vibethinker-3b-has-the-ai-world-arguing-over-benchmarks-again)). Frontier-class open MoEs remain datacenter-only, with Kimi K3's MXFP4 checkpoint alone at 1.56 TB, though SGLang's August 2026 "SSD Expert Pack" pages experts from disk to run DeepSeek-V4-Flash and K3 on consumer hardware ([LMSYS](https://www.lmsys.org/blog/2026-08-29-sglang-ssd-expert-pack)).

The training code has consolidated more than the models. GitHub API data pulled on 29 September shows six actively maintained RL-with-verifiable-rewards trainers, all pushed within two weeks and all Apache-2.0: **verl (23,687 stars)**, TRL (19,416), Unsloth (76,997), OpenRLHF (10,049), prime-rl (2,096) and SkyRL (2,364), plus the SGLang-side slime/Miles pair (8,559/3,020) that gave Kimi K3 day-zero RL, and NVIDIA NeMo-RL (2,037) ([verl](https://github.com/verl-project/verl); [TRL](https://github.com/huggingface/trl); [Unsloth](https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth); [OpenRLHF](https://github.com/OpenRLHF/OpenRLHF); [prime-rl](https://github.com/PrimeIntellect-ai/prime-rl); [SkyRL](https://github.com/NovaSky-AI/SkyRL); [slime](https://github.com/THUDM/slime); [Miles](https://github.com/radixark/miles)). GRPO and its stabilized variants (DAPO, Dr.GRPO, GSPO, CISPO, REINFORCE++) are the standard recipes and have been absorbed into defaults: a September 2026 Unsloth PR moved GRPO to TRL's DAPO loss ([PR #12088](https://github.com/unslothai/unsloth/pull/12088)), while the original DAPO repo has been frozen since May 2025 and the OpenThoughts data repo since December 2025. Hugging Face's Open-R1 (26,477 stars) remains the reference reproduction of the DeepSeek-R1 pipeline ([open-r1](https://github.com/huggingface/open-r1)). The practical division of labor is verl for multi-node RLVR beyond 30B, TRL or Unsloth for single-node fine-tuning, prime-rl or SkyRL for asynchronous agentic RL, and Miles for SGLang-centric trillion-scale MoE. Inference is equally settled: Ollama (181,898 stars), llama.cpp (129,838), vLLM (92,918) and SGLang (36,563), with HF TGI in maintenance mode ([VRLA Tech](https://vrlatech.com/llm-inference-engine-comparison-2026/), aggregator).

## Native thinking retired the scaffolds; equal-budget single agents beat debate

The most important 2026 fact about inference-time reasoning frameworks is that the model absorbed most of them. Anthropic's current prompting guide, which covers Fable 5.1 through Sonnet 5.5, lists manual chain-of-thought "as a fallback" for when thinking is off, states that "in internal evaluations, adaptive thinking reliably drives better performance than extended thinking," removes assistant prefill entirely from Claude 4.6 onward, and reports that "with adaptive thinking and subagent orchestration, Claude handles most multistep reasoning internally" ([Anthropic prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)). The same page documents a new failure mode of native reasoners, "overthinking and excessive thoroughness," to be fixed by lowering effort or instructing the model to "choose an approach and commit to it," and the steering page recommends per-message phrases such as "Please think hard before responding" on planning steps of agent harnesses ([Steering thinking](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking-steering-and-cost)). The surviving form of Self-Refine is Anthropic's explicit self-correction chain, draft → review against criteria → refine, which works because the review step has stated criteria and a separate call, not because models spontaneously self-correct; the 2023 result that intrinsic self-correction does not improve reasoning still stands ([arXiv 2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.01798)). The reference repositories for the 2023-era techniques confirm the shift: Tree of Thoughts (6,073 stars) last pushed January 2025, Reflexion (3,288) January 2025, OpenR's PRM-guided search January 2025, Self-Refine October 2024, and PAL June 2023 (GitHub API, 29 September 2026). DeepSeek's own R1 paper had already recorded that PRMs, MCTS and beam search never reached o1-class general reasoning ([arXiv 2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948v1)).

What still buys accuracy is expensive or offline. Self-consistency works but costs orders of magnitude: lifting Qwen3-8B from 68% to 82% on AIME 2025 by majority voting "requires 511 additional reasoning traces per question," 100 million extra tokens (snippet) ([arXiv 2508.15260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15260)). At matched compute, **sequential refinement beats parallel voting in 95.6% of configurations**, with inverse-entropy weighting of votes as a training-free improvement ([arXiv 2511.02309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02309), snippet). The largest documented gains now come from optimizing the scaffold offline: GEPA (ICLR 2026 oral) reports beating GRPO by 10% on average with 35x fewer rollouts and MIPROv2 on every benchmark, with self-reported jumps such as 67%→93% on MATH through its DSPy adapter and 32%→89% on an ARC-AGI agent ([gepa-ai/gepa](https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa); [Morph](https://www.morphllm.com/gepa-prompt-optimization)); DSPy itself stands at 38,411 stars and is pushed daily. A caveat for anyone planning to steer the model's reasoning by instruction: a 2026 study finds reasoning models' chain-of-thought controllability is "generally low (mostly below 10%)" (snippet) ([arXiv 2603.05706](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05706v1)), which means a SKILL.md can shape the visible procedure and outputs but should not be expected to reshape the hidden trace.

The evidence on multi-agent debate and councils is consistent and unflattering to "wisdom of crowds" on single-context reasoning. Under equal thinking-token budgets swept from 100 to 10,000 tokens across FRAMES and MuSiQue, three model families and five architectures, **single-agent systems "match or outperform multi-agent systems when computation is normalized"**, and the authors attribute reported multi-agent gains to compute and context effects rather than architecture ([arXiv 2604.02460](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02460v1); [Zhou review](https://www.zhongzhuzhou.org/blog/2026-05-18-singlevsmultiagent-technical-review-en/)). M3MAD-Bench (ACM Multimedia 2026; 13 datasets) finds "MAD is not uniformly effective," that collaborative LLM-Debate is the most robust variant while adversarial Div-MAD "often underperformed even single-agent baselines," and that multi-perspective benefit "is minimal or even negative in unimodal tasks" ([arXiv 2601.02854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02854), snippet). Anthropic's own numbers frame the exception: its Opus-lead/Sonnet-subagent research system beat single-agent Opus 4 by 90.2% on an internal eval, but "token usage by itself explains 80% of the variance," multi-agent systems use "about 15× more tokens than chats," and they are poor "for domains requiring all agents to share the same context" ([Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)); its January 2026 guidance adds that teams "invest months building elaborate multi-agent architectures only to discover that improved prompting on a single agent achieved equivalent results" ([claude.com blog](https://claude.com/blog/building-multi-agent-systems-when-and-how-to-use-them)). The defensible use of a council pattern is therefore breadth-first search, context isolation, or multimodal inputs, not reasoning per se. Note that no study compares a council of frontier models against one of them at `max` effort; the equal-budget work used open-weight models and Gemini 2.5.

For the harness itself, the Claude Agent SDK is the only framework whose reasoning scaffold (adaptive and interleaved thinking, native subagent spawning, hooks that "cannot be reasoned around") is co-designed with the model; its 8,185-star Python repo understates adoption because most use flows through the 148,522-star Claude Code harness it wraps ([claude-agent-sdk-python](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-agent-sdk-python); [claude-code](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code); [Boringbot](https://boringbot.substack.com/p/claude-code-skills-subagents-hooks)). The model-agnostic choices with daily pushes and MIT licences are LangGraph (42,448 stars), the OpenAI Agents SDK (29,755) and Pydantic AI (20,251); CrewAI (59,158) and Google ADK (21,675) are active; AutoGen (61,215) has been in maintenance mode since 2 October 2025 with Microsoft Agent Framework 1.0 (April 2026) and the AG2 fork as successors ([Atlan](https://atlan.com/know/ai-agent/what-is-autogen/), snippet). Projects that aim at *human-like* deliberation rather than accuracy are tiny and academic: yale-nlp's metacognition survey repo (70 stars), a Bayesian meta-reasoning repo (16 stars, no licence), a dual-process ablation repo created on 24 September 2026 with zero stars, and the stale system-2-research link collection (last push March 2025); Karpathy's llm-council three-stage pattern is the only widely used "deliberation" scaffold and it targets answer quality with no benchmark ([yale-nlp/LLM-Metacognition](https://github.com/yale-nlp/LLM-Metacognition); [karpathy/llm-council](https://github.com/karpathy/llm-council)). A skills-based reasoning toolkit is building in largely empty space.

## Reasoning skill packs are broad, tiny, and unevaluated, including this one

The Agent Skills format that skills-for-humanity targets is now an open standard with real cross-host reach. Anthropic released it on 18 December 2025 and governance sits with the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation (secondary sources; [Strapi](https://strapi.io/blog/what-are-agent-skills-and-how-to-use-them); [Agentailor](https://blog.agentailor.com/posts/top-ai-agent-standards-2026)); the 25.8k-star spec repo requires only `name` (max 64 chars, lowercase/hyphens) and `description` (max 1024 chars), recommends instructions under 5,000 tokens and SKILL.md under 500 lines, and ships a `skills-ref validate` tool ([agentskills/agentskills](https://github.com/agentskills/agentskills); [specification.mdx](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/agentskills/agentskills/main/docs/specification.mdx)). Claude Code extends it with `disable-model-invocation`, `allowed-tools`, `context` and `agent` fields and has merged custom slash commands into skills ([Claude Code skills docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills)); OpenAI Codex scans `.agents/skills` and invokes via `$skill` ([Codex skills docs](https://developers.openai.com/codex/skills)); Vercel's `npx skills add` installs any GitHub SKILL.md into "20+ AI coding agents" ([skills.sh](https://www.skills.sh/)). Because reasoning skills are almost always pure markdown with no scripts, they are the most portable kind of skill in the ecosystem. The unit of distribution is drifting toward bundles: `openai/skills` (27.8k stars) is "now deprecated in favor of the OpenAI Plugins repository" ([openai/skills](https://github.com/openai/skills)), and AAIF has published an "Agent Plugins 1.0" post (blocked; existence only).

The center of gravity of the ecosystem is software delivery, not thinking. The large collections are `anthropics/skills` (178.9k stars; docx, pdf, mcp-builder, frontend-design), `obra/superpowers` (292.6k; TDD, systematic debugging, verification-before-completion), BMAD-METHOD (53.6k; agile workflow) and VoltAgent's awesome list (35k stars, 1,497+ skills organized by vendor with **no reasoning or decision-making category**) ([anthropics/skills](https://github.com/anthropics/skills); [obra/superpowers](https://github.com/obra/superpowers); [BMAD-METHOD](https://github.com/bmad-code-org/BMAD-METHOD); [VoltAgent](https://github.com/VoltAgent/awesome-agent-skills)). The only official Anthropic skill in the epistemics space is `discernment-nudge`, a single behavior that appends two or three verification questions after substantive answers ([discernment-nudge](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/discernment-nudge/SKILL.md)). BMAD embeds the largest catalogue of named thinking techniques found anywhere, 108 elicitation and brainstorming methods including Six Thinking Hats, Delphi and Socratic pressure-testing, plus a multi-persona "party mode" with an anti-consensus persona group, but packages them as workflow steps inside its agents rather than as standalone skills ([BMAD releases](https://github.com/bmad-code-org/BMAD-METHOD/releases)). General reasoning packs are two orders of magnitude smaller and mostly single-maintainer.

On breadth, skills-for-humanity is the most encyclopedic project in its niche: upstream reports 34 domains and 200+ methods drawing on de Bono, Meadows, Altshuller, Minto, Goldratt, Tetlock, Klein, Rawls, Nash, Axelrod, Ostrom, Shannon and Kahneman, with each skill pausing "to state what it's analyzing and ask how deep to go" ([human-avatar/skills-for-humanity](https://github.com/human-avatar/skills-for-humanity)), and it is the one reasoning pack filed in a major awesome-list ([BehiSecc/awesome-claude-skills](https://github.com/BehiSecc/awesome-claude-skills)). The local working copy differs from upstream in ways worth knowing: it holds **171 skill directories across 27 categories** with a `/think` router and per-category routing commands and no `s4h-` prefix, so it is a variant rather than a mirror (local filesystem observation, 29 September 2026). Its closest competitor, cc-thinking-skills, is more pragmatic (functional stages, an explicit router and combination skill, four-host support) and is the only reasoning pack with any measured result, and that result is a provisional "+4.0 percentage points" for a single skill that the authors themselves call "below the +5 point utility margin" with "evidence gaps," the remaining 27 skills being "manual-only" with "zero model calls and an unmeasured result" ([cc-thinking-skills](https://github.com/tjboudreaux/cc-thinking-skills)). The shared weakness across all three packs is identical: instructions-only markdown with no published evidence of reasoning uplift.

That gap is now cheap to close, because the evaluation paradigm has settled and the tooling exists. Anthropic's skill-creator gained evals in March 2026 (per-case subagents, `benchmark.json` "for with-skill versus without-skill," blind A/B comparator agents, description tuning that "improved triggering on 5 out of 6 public skills") ([claude.com blog](https://claude.com/blog/improving-skill-creator-test-measure-and-refine-agent-skills)), and **`claude plugin eval` (Claude Code v2.1.269+, September 2026)** runs each case three times in a with-arm and a no-plugin without-arm, reports the delta, offers six grader types of which four (`regex`, `tool_used`, `tool_order`, `file_exists`) cost nothing and two (`llm`, `baseline`) call a judge, and exposes exit codes for CI ([Claude Code plugin evals docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-evals)). The docs warn that the most common first finding is "a Δ near zero with the case's `tool_used: Skill` grader failing, which means Claude isn't choosing your skill on natural phrasing," which is exactly the routing question a 27-category toolkit needs answered first. The academic evidence supports uplift for procedural skills and is silent on reasoning skills: SkillsBench (86 tasks, 11 domains) finds curated skills raise average pass rate by 16%, from +4.5 points in software engineering to +51.9 in healthcare, while **"self-generated skills offer no benefit"**; SkillJuror finds skill organization shifts uptake sharply but yields only +4.1% aggregate and that effective skill writing "is not merely about brevity, but about actionable routing"; SkillEval's document-level quality score predicts observed uplift at r≈0.78 (all snippet-level; arXiv blocked) ([SkillsBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12670); [SkillJuror](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.11543); [SkillEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06891)). No controlled study measuring whether mental-model or critical-thinking SKILL.md packs improve argument validity, calibration or decision quality against a no-skill baseline was found; the question is open as of 29 September 2026, and whichever pack first ships a credible eval suite differentiates itself from a field of near-identical 21-, 28-, 39- and 98-model variants.

## Post-training makes models less human, so fidelity needs base weights and behavioral data

If the goal is to reproduce *how humans reason* rather than to reason well, the evidence points away from chat models entirely. **Centaur** (Binz et al., Nature, August 2025), a Llama-3.1-70B fine-tuned on Psych-101's trial-by-trial data from more than 60,000 participants making over 10 million choices across 160 experiments, outperformed domain-specific cognitive models in all but one of the 160 experiments (average NLL 0.13 lower than cognitive models), generalized to held-out participants, new cover stories and new domains, and predicted reaction times (search summaries; Nature blocked) ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09215-4); [Helmholtz Munich](https://www.helmholtz-munich.de/en/newsroom/news-all/artikel/ai-that-thinks-like-us-and-could-help-explain-how-we-think)). Its weights and LoRA adapter are public under the Llama 3.1 licence ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/marcelbinz/Llama-3.1-Centaur-70B)). Three follow-on results reshape how to use it. First, the COLM 2026 "Centauri" study shows **models of 0.6B–1B parameters suffice to match a reproduced Centaur-70B in-distribution**, with steeper scaling out-of-distribution, and releases MIT-licensed code plus 117 LoRA adapters across Llama, Qwen3, OLMo and SmolLM bases ([socius-org/Centauri](https://github.com/socius-org/Centauri); [arXiv 2608.05224](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.05224)). Second, the Apache-2.0 **Psych-201** successor dataset accepts pull-request contributions and adds reaction-time, age, diagnosis and nationality fields, with search summaries citing 208,021 participants and 25.9 million responses ([Psych-201](https://github.com/marcelbinz/Psych-201); [arXiv 2605.07632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07632)). Third, and most consequential for a Claude-based builder, the Binz group's May 2026 paper finds that **post-training "consistently reduces alignment with human behavior across model families, sizes, and objectives,"** that base models keep improving in behavioral alignment across generations while the post-training gap widens, and that persona induction "does not improve predictions at the level of individuals" (search summary) ([arXiv 2605.07632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07632)). By this metric, Claude, GPT and Gemini chat models are worse models of human behavior than their own base checkpoints.

Centaur's fidelity claim is contested on exactly the axis a "simulator" cares about. Bowers argues it "can't explain anything about human cognition" and reports two manipulations under which it "failed dramatically, in ways that show that Centaur's predictions are completely unrelated to the processes that drive human responses" ([Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/researchers-claim-their-ai-model-simulates-human-mind-others-are-skeptical); [Bowers](https://bsky.app/profile/jeffreybowers.bsky.social/post/3lsjxpcfetv2n), search summaries); a 2026 JEP commentary calls it "brittle and sensitive to small variations in task input" ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41021501/)); and a National Science Open paper names the "behaviourist trap," concluding Centaur is "a high-coverage behavioral emulator for tasks encoded in natural language" that does not simulate timing dynamics, attention, physical interaction or social settings ([NSO 2026](https://www.nso-journal.org/articles/nso/pdf/2026/01/NSO20250053.pdf)). The individual-level picture from the digital-twin literature is the same shape. Stanford's interview-grounded generative agents of 1,052 real people replicate their General Social Survey answers **"85% as accurately as participants replicate their own answers two weeks later"** ([arXiv 2411.10109](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)), and the MIT-licensed `genagents` code is public while the 1,000-agent bank is access-controlled ([genagents](https://github.com/joonspk-research/genagents)). But a September 2026 study finds twins "can reproduce average human effects while providing little information about which individuals differ from those averages," and that newer models and richer respondent information "do not reliably translate into human-data savings" ([arXiv 2609.07987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07987)); SILICA's agent-society benchmark finds agreement with humans "is confined to starting points," with no model matching end-state cooperation in public-goods games ([arXiv 2608.28182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28182)); and Twin-2K-500 reports digital twins replicating 6 of 10 behavioral-economics results that humans themselves replicated across waves ([Marketing Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2025.0262)). Nature Machine Intelligence documented that LLM stand-ins "harmfully misportray and flatten identity groups" across 3,200 participants and 16 identities ([Nature MI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-00986-z)). The consistent pattern is high aggregate coverage, weak individual and out-of-distribution fidelity, and no established process fidelity.

The signature-level evidence that LLM reasoning resembles human reasoning is real but must not be read as mechanism. DeepSeek-R1's reasoning-token counts predict human reaction times across seven task types with **r = 0.39–0.89 (mean 0.62)** versus r = 0.44 for the base V3 model ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2520077122); [MIT McGovern](https://mcgovern.mit.edu/2025/11/19/the-cost-of-thinking/)), and a follow-up finds the reasoning budget does not modulate that alignment ("Effort as Ceiling, Not Dial") ([arXiv 2605.16938](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16938), title/summary). Language models show human-like content effects on syllogisms and the Wason task down to the relation between answer distributions and human response times ([PNAS Nexus](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/3/7/pgae233/7712372)), and GPT-4 matches or exceeds 1,907 humans on false belief, irony and hints while falling short on faux pas because of "a hyperconservative approach towards committing to conclusions" ([Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-01882-z)). Against that, a 2026 anatomy of mathematical reasoning finds human solutions "maintain a compact alternation between analysis and deduction, whereas DeepSeek-R1 frequently revisits intermediate results and performs shallow verification without meaningful logical progress" ([arXiv 2606.07410](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07410)), semantic-memory structure appears "irreducible only in humans" ([arXiv 2604.12816](https://awesomepapers.io/ai-agents/papers/2604.12816)), and the Nature Reviews Psychology dual-process review concludes LLM reasoning "is not fully analogous to human dual-process cognition" and that LLMs "exhibit specific non-human biases, such as hallucination" ([Nature Reviews Psychology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-025-00506-1)). A simulator that shows its work as a human-like alternation of analysis and deduction will have to impose that structure from the outside, which is precisely what a methodology skill does.

The classical cognitive architectures survive as process-level references with confirmed code in two cases. **Soar 9.6.5 shipped on 6 May 2026** under BSD-2 with Python, Java, C# and JavaScript bindings ([SoarGroup/Soar releases](https://github.com/SoarGroup/Soar/releases)); ACT-R's reference manual is at 7.30+ with a GPL-3 Python port, pyactr, that covers symbolic and subsymbolic processes ([pyactr](https://github.com/jakdot/pyactr)); the Common Model of Cognition group is actively extending the shared model with metacognition (2025) ([arXiv 2506.07807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07807)). CLARION exists as a "highly experimental" Python port, and LIDA and Sigma show no confirmed 2026 code activity. Hybrid work flows in one direction, grafting ACT-R memory activation and decision traces into LLM agents (LLM-ACTR reports better representation of human decision behavior than a chain-of-thought baseline on a manufacturing task) ([AAAI-SS](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI-SS/article/view/35610)); a July 2026 mechanism-level review notes that CLARION's explicit/implicit coordination and LIDA's competition-before-broadcast are execution organizations "language agents have not migrated" ([arXiv 2607.23942](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23942v1), search summary). The Stanford Smallville code (22.2k stars, Apache-2.0) and Humanoid Agents (System-1 needs and emotions layered on generative agents, 324 stars) remain the open reference points for agent societies, neither validated against human behavioral data in their READMEs ([generative_agents](https://github.com/joonspk-research/generative_agents); [HumanoidAgents](https://github.com/HumanoidAgents/HumanoidAgents)).

## Benchmarks measure five different "human baselines", and humans still win on novelty

"Human-level" is only meaningful relative to how the baseline was built, and 2026 benchmarks use at least five constructions: panel solvability (ARC-AGI-2 and 3, where every task was solved by at least two people, giving "100%"), mean individual accuracy of a small sample (ARC-AGI-2 average human 66%; SimpleBench 83.7% from nine people; MMMU expert ceiling 88.6%), domain-expert accuracy (GPQA Diamond ~70%), a theoretical good-strategy figure (Vending-Bench 2's "$63k in a year"), and head-to-head expert preference (GDPval, Remote Labor Index) ([arcprize/ARC-AGI-2](https://github.com/arcprize/ARC-AGI-2); [It Does What Now](https://itdoeswhatnow.com/benchmarks/simplebench/); [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/gpqa-diamond-ai-benchmark); [Andon Labs](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2)). ARC-AGI-2 illustrates the ambiguity in one benchmark: models have surpassed the 66% individual mean since spring 2026 yet Chollet maintains they "are not yet human-level on an efficiency basis" ([Chollet on X](https://x.com/fchollet/status/1999183528492433672)). Frontier models now exceed the baseline wherever the test rewards stored knowledge plus short derivation: the GPQA lead over PhD experts has grown from about 7 points in 2024 to about 24 in 2026 with the top models "clustered within 1.2 points" ([AIToolTier](https://aitooltier.com/benchmarks/gpqa-diamond)); ARC-AGI-1 is "effectively performance-saturated" ([Kamradt](https://x.com/GregKamradt/status/2021997415436558451)); and on Watson & Holmes, the one 2026 benchmark with matched-condition human calibration on a naturalistic detective task, reasoning models moved from the lower human quartile to "approximately the top 5%" over nine months (o3-pro 2.14, GPT-5 2.10 vs human mean 1.43) ([arXiv 2602.19914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.19914)).

Humans still clearly win where the structure is novelty at test time, interaction over many steps, or adversarially simple distractors. **ARC-AGI-3**, launched 25 March 2026 as turn-based novel environments scored by relative human action efficiency with a budget of five times the human median, opened with humans at 100% and frontier models between 0% and 0.37% ([ARC-AGI-3 technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621v1); [ARC-AGI-3-Agents](https://github.com/arcprize/ARC-AGI-3-Agents)); the September aggregator figure of 62.7% for Astra could not be confirmed against arcprize.org, and a competing 99.9% claim under OpenAI's own harness almost certainly belongs to the separate community leaderboard that permits custom harnesses ([llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/blog/research/gpt-6-astra-launch); [ARC-AGI-3](https://arcprize.org/arc-agi/3)). On **SimpleBench** the best model, Claude Fable, scored 81.9% in August 2026 against the 83.7% human baseline ([SimpleBench](https://simple-bench.com/); [BenchmarkList](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/simplebench/)); on **Vending-Bench 2** the September leader (GPT-6 Astra, $15,514 end-of-year balance, aggregator) sits near a quarter of Andon Labs' $63k good-human estimate ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/vendingbench2); [Andon Labs](https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench-2)); on the **Remote Labor Index** automation rates were 0.8–2.5% (date unclear) ([Scale Labs RLI](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/rli)). METR's time-horizon trend tells the same story from a different angle: the 50% horizon reached "likely at least 16 hours" for Claude Mythos Preview in May 2026 with an 89-day doubling time since 2024, but **the 80% horizon was only 3h06m**, and METR says measurements above 16 hours are unreliable on its current suite ([METR dashboard](https://metr.org/time-horizons/); [METR Time Horizon 1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/), snippets). The International AI Safety Report 2026 endorses this "evaluation gap" framing, noting models that reach IMO gold "still fail at elementary mathematical calculations and struggle to infer basic causal relationships" ([International AI Safety Report 2026](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026)).

Three measurement problems should temper any leaderboard reading. Item quality in the flagship "hard" benchmarks is poor: FutureHouse found about **29% of HLE text-only chemistry and biology answers "incorrect or misleading"** (the HLE team's own re-audit found ~18%), tracing the cause to a selection rule that kept only questions frontier models failed with five minutes of review each ([FutureHouse](https://www.futurehouse.org/research/hle-exam)), and Epoch's 12 June 2026 FrontierMath revision fixed **errors in 42% of problems** ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/epoch-frontiermath-v2-error-corrected-ai-benchmark-analysis)), so 10–20 point movements sit inside item-error noise. Harness dependence is roughly 10–15 points for the same model and benchmark (HLE for Fable 5.1 ranges 59.1 to 65.6 across sources; FrontierMath 87.8% Epoch-run vs 97.6% lab-reported), so every score needs its harness named. And calibration remains a live gap, with HLE calibration errors of 34–89% and 2026 work noting reasoning models "may be confidently wrong" ([arXiv 2604.23333](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23333v1)). Theory-of-mind benchmarks carry a construct-validity critique of their own: Riemer et al. argue most "are broken" because they measure literal prediction of behavior while assuming a self-consistency LLMs lack, and find open models "struggle with functional theory of mind — even with exceedingly simple partner policies" ([arXiv 2412.19726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.19726)); CogToM (8,000+ instances, 46 paradigms, 22 models) reports "potential divergences between LLM and human cognitive structures" ([arXiv 2601.15628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15628)). For self-evaluation, the maintained general harness is Inspect with `inspect_evals` (MIT; covers BBEH and ARC among others), with EleutherAI's lm-evaluation-harness for open weights and HELM in maintenance mode since 1 June 2026; ARC-AGI-3 and tau2-bench (MIT, v1.0.1) require their own repos because they are interactive, and only METR's `eval-analysis-public` and the ARC datasets ship human-baseline data alongside code ([inspect_evals](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_evals); [lm-evaluation-harness](https://github.com/EleutherAI/lm-evaluation-harness); [HELM](https://github.com/stanford-crfm/helm); [tau2-bench](https://github.com/sierra-research/tau2-bench); [METR](https://github.com/METR/eval-analysis-public)).

## Recommended stack: one for winning, one for being human

The evidence supports two distinct builds on top of Claude Code skills, and the honest recommendation is to pick one explicitly, because the choices conflict at the model layer.

**Stack A, best at reasoning tasks.** Use Claude Opus 5.5 as the default subject model and Claude Fable 5.1 at `high` or `xhigh` effort for the hardest reasoning and long-horizon work, following Anthropic's own routing rule ([Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)); Opus 5.5 leads Astra by 10 points on HLE and 300 Elo on GDPval-AA on Anthropic's harness at 40% of the list price ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)). Run it through Claude Code or the Claude Agent SDK, which is the only harness whose interleaved thinking, subagent spawning and hooks are co-designed with the model, and default to a **single adaptive-thinking agent**, escalating to subagents only for breadth-first search, context isolation or multimodal inputs, per the equal-budget and Anthropic evidence above. Use skills-for-humanity as the *methodology* layer, not a search layer: the category routers and `/think` do the diagnosis, and each skill imposes the explicit procedure and output structure that the hidden trace will not show. Steer depth per step with effort and phrases like "think hard before responding" on planning steps ([Steering thinking](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/thinking-steering-and-cost)), and encode any rule that must hold (a premortem before a decision skill returns, a source check before a claim) as a hook rather than a prompt, since hooks "cannot be reasoned around." Optimize skill descriptions and router prompts offline with GEPA or DSPy against a held-out eval set, the one scaffold-level lever with large documented deltas. Evaluate in three tiers: `claude plugin eval` with the no-plugin baseline arm for every category router (the routing question comes first, per SkillJuror and Anthropic's own docs); Inspect for BBEH, GPQA Diamond and a private SimpleBench-style set of everyday trick questions; and the ARC-AGI-3 benchmarking repo plus Watson & Holmes for novelty and naturalistic reasoning. Where abstract puzzle performance matters, cross-check against GPT-6 Astra through a model-agnostic harness (LangGraph or Pydantic AI), since Astra leads ARC-AGI-2/3 and FrontierMath by margins no Claude model matches. For an open-weight fallback with no licence conditions, GLM-5.3-Flash (MIT) or Qwen3.8-27B (Apache 2.0) on vLLM, and Qwen3.5-9B on llama.cpp or Ollama for a laptop.

**Stack B, most faithful to human reasoning.** Do not use a chat model as the subject. Take an open *base* checkpoint (the Centauri recipe covers Llama 3.1/3.2, Qwen3, OLMo and SmolLM bases from 0.6B to 14B, MIT code) and LoRA-fine-tune it on Psych-101 plus Psych-201 transcripts using TRL or Unsloth, training only on the `<<response>>` tokens as the Psych-201 README specifies; a 1B–8B model is enough in-distribution and the larger sizes buy out-of-distribution transfer ([Centauri](https://github.com/socius-org/Centauri); [Psych-201](https://github.com/marcelbinz/Psych-201)). Add individual-level grounding with `genagents`-style interview memory (MIT) for the specific people or personas being simulated, and, if process-level dynamics matter (memory decay, retrieval latency, production-rule conflict), wrap the model with pyactr (GPL-3, a licensing consideration for distribution) or Soar (BSD-2). Serve the fine-tuned model with vLLM or llama.cpp. Claude and the skills then take the role of the *experimenter and orchestrator*, not the subject: skills drive the experimental protocol, perturbation tests and write-up, and where a "System 2" deliberation trace is required for the simulated human, a methodology skill running on Claude can generate it, but that trace should be labelled as scaffold rather than as fidelity. Evaluate against the constructs the literature actually validates: held-out negative log-likelihood on Psych-201 tasks, test-retest ceilings from Twin-2K-500-style repeated waves, SILICA-style rule-preserving perturbations and anti-memorization variants, reaction-time correlation, faux-pas and CogToM theory-of-mind items, and functional rather than literal theory of mind. Treat raw task accuracy as a non-goal, and expect good aggregate fit with weak individual-level and out-of-distribution fit as the current state of the art.

Where the evidence for these recommendations is thin, it is thin in specific, nameable places. The research sandbox could not open openai.com, deepmind.google, x.ai, arcprize.org, epoch.ai, artificialanalysis.ai, labs.scale.com, metr.org, arxiv.org, huggingface.co, nature.com, science.org or agentskills.io; only anthropic.com, platform.claude.com, code.claude.com, claude.com and GitHub were read in full. Consequently every GPT-6, Gemini and Grok specification and price, every ARC Prize, Epoch, Artificial Analysis and Scale leaderboard number, the Centaur/Psych-201 statistics, the SkillsBench/SkillJuror/SkillEval effect sizes, the METR horizon figures and the licence clauses of Kimi K3 and GLM-5.3 are second-hand from search snippets or aggregators such as BenchLM, llm-stats and morphllm. Several conflicts remain unresolved for the same reason: the AA index "53 vs 53" and "58.9 vs 57.6" snapshots appear to be on different scales; Nemotron 3 Ultra's AA score is reported as both 48 and 23; Qwen3.8-Max's weights date is 3 or 12 August; the 62.7% ARC-AGI-3 figure for Astra conflicts with a benchmarks-side finding that nothing above 1% could be verified; and Anthropic's own two tables give Fable 5.1 different HLE and GDPval-AA values (65.0 vs 65.6; 1853 vs 1735) across harness versions. No study head-to-heads self-consistency, debate and native thinking on a current frontier API model, no study measures reasoning-skill uplift, and no independent Epoch or ARC Prize scores were found for Opus 5.5 beyond ARC-AGI-2, for Sonnet 5.5 or for Mythos 5.1. Anyone acting on the numbers in the model and benchmark tables should open the primary pages first.

## Conclusion

The landscape has inverted an assumption that most "human-level reasoning simulator" projects still carry: that the path to human-like reasoning runs through the strongest reasoner. In September 2026 the strongest reasoners hide their reasoning, cannot have their traces steered by prompt, and are, by the one direct measurement available, drifting further from human behavior with every round of post-training; meanwhile the systems that best predict human choices are small, base-weight models fine-tuned on behavioral transcripts that no one claims embody a mechanism. The unifying implication for a skills-based project is that the SKILL.md layer is no longer a way to make the model think better (native adaptive thinking already does that) but the only place where a *legible, human-shaped procedure* can be imposed and inspected, which is a different and more defensible product than a prompt pack.

That reframing also tells the reader what to do next with their own repository. The comparative advantage of skills-for-humanity is breadth and lineage; its shared liability with every other reasoning pack is zero evidence. With `claude plugin eval` shipping a with/without baseline in the same month, and the academic finding that routing organization matters more than skill prose, the highest-value experiment is a routing-first ablation across the 27 category commands using cheap transcript graders, followed by a small set of open-ended items graded against human-calibrated benchmarks such as Watson & Holmes. Whoever publishes that delta first, positive or null, will own the reasoning-skill niche that no awesome-list yet has a category for.
